How to Build the LEGO Crane

Eleven steps, built from the baseplate upward — the same eight parts named on the mosaic: baseplate, legs, body, wings, neck, head, beak, and crown.

These steps are a reconstruction, not a record. They were worked out from a photograph of the finished bird, so the piece sizes are a plausible way to reach that shape rather than the exact pieces that were used. Substitute freely for whatever is in the bin — that substitution is the design work, not a compromise.

Pieces you will need

1 green baseplate, 12 × 6
6 black 1 × 1 bricks
5 light gray 1 × 1 bricks
1 white 2 × 6 plate
1 white 2 × 6 brick
1 white 2 × 4 brick
1 white 2 × 2 brick
2 white 2 × 1 slopes
2 light gray 2 × 4 plates
2 black 2 × 2 bricks
1 white 1 × 1 round tile
1 red 2 × 1 slope
1 yellow 2 × 2 plate
5 yellow upright pieces
1

Start with the baseplate

Everything stands on one green baseplate. Set it down with the long side facing you — the crane will face to the right.

Add these pieces
1 green baseplate, 12 × 6
2

Stand up the two legs

Stack three 1 × 1 bricks for each leg, two studs apart. Thin legs are the most fragile part of the whole bird — press every brick fully home.

Add these pieces
6 black 1 × 1 bricks
3

Bridge the legs with a plate

One long plate ties the legs together and becomes the underside of the body. This is the piece that stops the legs wobbling.

Add these pieces
1 white 2 × 6 plate
4

Build the body's first course

One long brick on top of the plate. The body needs real bulk — a bird built only of plates reads as a paper cutout standing on edge.

Add these pieces
1 white 2 × 6 brick
5

Slot the wings in at the sides

A plate each side, overlapping the body by one row of studs. They go on now, mid-body, so the next course pins them down — that is what makes them read as wings at the bird's flanks rather than a slab laid over its back.

Add these pieces
2 light gray 2 × 4 plates
6

Second course, and taper the tail

The next course pins the wings and steps in at the back, where a slope finishes the tail. This is where the block starts to read as a bird rather than a box.

Add these pieces
1 white 2 × 4 brick
1 white 2 × 1 slope
7

Close the back and seat the neck

A 2 × 2 brick at the front gives the neck something solid to stand on; one more slope rounds off the back.

Add these pieces
1 white 2 × 2 brick
1 white 2 × 1 slope
8

Raise the neck

Five 1 × 1 bricks stacked straight up. The neck is the crane's signature — and at one brick wide it is also the piece most likely to snap. Pick the finished bird up by its baseplate, never by the neck.

Add these pieces
5 light gray 1 × 1 bricks
9

Set the head on top

Two 2 × 2 bricks make the head, overhanging the neck toward the front. A white round tile on the side is the eye — one small piece that does more for the bird than any other.

Add these pieces
2 black 2 × 2 bricks
1 white 1 × 1 round tile
10

Point the beak forward

One red slope at the front of the head is the beak. The mosaic could only spell this out as a few red tiles; in brick it becomes a single wedge — a shape the 8 mm grid could never hold.

Add these pieces
1 red 2 × 1 slope
11

Finish with the golden crown

A yellow plate across the head carries the crown. Stand short pieces upright in a fan, tallest in the middle — this is the detail that names the bird, so spread them wide.

Add these pieces
1 yellow 2 × 2 plate
5 yellow upright pieces
When it is standing. Set the finished bird beside the mosaic it came from and compare them: the neck too short, the body too narrow, the crown too tight. Every difference you can name is the next revision. Because the bricks come apart as easily as they go together, changing your mind costs nothing — which is exactly what makes a prototype worth building.
